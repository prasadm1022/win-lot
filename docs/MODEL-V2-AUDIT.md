## Summary in plain language

- **The honest replay matches chance.** Over 1,334 past draws the Top pick matched 0.2016 numbers per draw; a random ticket matches 0.2029. That is the same as random; the difference is luck.
- **Real jackpot evidence behaves like random evidence.** Replacing the real jackpot combinations (100 replicates) or the older jackpot dates (60 replicates) with random ones gives results in the same range. After allowing for the 10 comparisons, the smallest corrected p-value is 0.99, so nothing stands out. Dhana Nidhanaya's real hits sit at percentile 0.08 of its placebos (low, but within what luck produces across this many comparisons).
- **With fair random draws the hits stay at chance** (pooled hits z -0.66, rank z +0.80, every lottery within 2 standard errors). The Top pick then holds +0.33 past jackpot numbers per draw more than a random ticket (SE 0.10): positive by design (jackpot decades), capped by the guards. It favours the tens where jackpot numbers fell; it is not a sign of foresight.
- **The copy guards stay small as evidence grows.** From 0 to 100 synthetic jackpot combinations they never exclude more than 3.83% of all tickets, and a Top pick that keeps the owner's order was found at every step: yes.
- **The 24-number search loses nothing.** For every lottery the Top pick is also the best rule-passing ticket over all tickets.
- **No constant is fragile.** Changing any constant by 50% either way moves the pooled hits between 0.1972 and 0.2099; every variant stays at chance. The constants are frozen policy and are never retuned on these results.
- Every ticket has the same chance in a fair draw. Jackpot data describes the players, not the machine.

## 3. Null simulation

10 replicates per lottery: every draw's numbers are replaced by a fair random ticket (seeded), keeping the real dates, letters, bonus numbers and jackpot items (combinations take the synthetic numbers of their draw). The whole walk-forward is rerun. Mean hits and rank percentile use their analytic chance levels; the copy tilt (Top-pick numbers from past jackpots minus a random ticket's, per step) uses the spread between replicates, because consecutive steps share most of their evidence. The guard column is the most numbers any null Top pick shared with one past jackpot / took from all of them, checked at every step against that step's own copy-guard rung (docs/MODEL-V2.md section 2.14).
